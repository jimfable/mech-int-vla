\section*{Executive summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive summary}

\textbf{Question.} A robot \emph{policy}---the neural network that turns camera images, the robot's joint readings and a text instruction into motor commands---sometimes fails at its task. Do the network's \emph{internal activations} (the intermediate vectors it computes on the way to its output) tell you that a failure is coming, beyond what you could already know from the policy's \emph{outputs} and from \emph{privileged simulator state} (the true positions of everything, which a simulator knows but a real robot does not)? If yes, reading the internals of such vision-language-action (VLA) models buys something. If no, it does not---at least not in this form.

\textbf{What we did.} We froze one public policy (SmolVLA fine-tuned on LIBERO), one manipulation task (``pick up the book and place it in the back compartment of the caddy''), and three nested failure predictors: \Mz{} (policy outputs only), \Mo{} (\Mz{} + privileged simulator state), \Mt{} (\Mo{} + internal signals built from a probe for the book's relative orientation). Every threshold, metric, and analysis step was preregistered and hash-locked before the held-out \emph{Locked Test} split (160 episodes, 8 perturbation conditions) was collected. The evaluator ran exactly once. Cost: 21.7 GPU-hours on one RTX~5090.

\textbf{Results (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The preregistered primary claim fails, and fails cleanly.} \Mt{} beats \Mo{} in paired log loss by $+0.47\%$ (90\% cluster-bootstrap CI $[-1.2\%,\,+2.1\%]$; bar: $\geq 3\%$). The interval excludes the bar, but only by 0.9 percentage points: a 3\% lift is unlikely, a 1--2\% lift is not ruled out.
\item \textbf{Privileged state (with or without internals) beats outputs alone.} \Mt{} beats the output-only baseline \Mz{} by $18.8\%$ in log loss ($\Delta$ log loss $-0.120$, CI $[-0.187,\,-0.052]$, both ends favourable); AUROC $0.667 \to 0.835$. Because \Mt{} contains all of \Mo{}'s state features, this gap is carried by the simulator state; whether internals \emph{alone} could replace that state (a monitor ``outputs + internals'') was not one of the preregistered models and remains untested.
\item \textbf{The decodable orientation direction is a readout, not a lever.} A linear \emph{probe} can read the book's orientation out of the activations. But when we \emph{edit} the activations along that probe direction (``patching''), the policy's action moves \emph{less} than when we edit along a random direction of the same size (median off-target ratio $4.0$; bar $\leq 0.25$), and in the predicted direction in only $48\%$ of 52 valid cases (CI $[36\%,62\%]$; bar: CI above $50\%$). The same holds at larger edit sizes and in every perturbation condition.
\item \textbf{No earlier alarm.} At a fixed 10\% episode-level false-positive rate, \Mt{} and \Mo{} detect 98\% of the 60 failures with identical median lead time (paired difference $0$ steps, CI $[0,0]$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Takeaway.} On this policy and task, the internal orientation signal adds nothing to what the privileged state already says about upcoming failure, and the direction we can decode is not one the policy acts through. Being able to \emph{read} a variable from a network is not the same as the network \emph{using} it. Per the preregistered decision table this is the ``neither lift nor specificity'' row: publish the negative result, stop the line.

\textbf{Post-hoc addendum (Section~\ref{sec:explore}).} On the Calibration split, a linear read-out of the raw activation vector, without any simulator state, ranks failures about as well as \Mo{} (AUROC 0.94 vs 0.95). Internals can largely \emph{stand in} for privileged state even though they add nothing \emph{on top} of it; this was not preregistered and is not part of the confirmatory result.

\textbf{Confidence.} High that the measured numbers are what this protocol produces (single frozen run; every input content-addressed). Moderate that the conclusion generalises: one policy, one task, one layer, 158 valid episodes. Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists what would change our mind, with rough probabilities.

\section{The setting in plain terms}
\label{sec:plain}
This section defines every object the rest of the report uses. Readers who work on VLA models can skip to Section~\ref{sec:why}.

\subsection{The robot and its policy}
The robot is a simulated 7-joint arm with a gripper in the LIBERO benchmark. Its \textbf{policy} $\pi$ is a neural network with about 450 million parameters (SmolVLA). At every control step $t$ it receives an \emph{observation} $o_t$---two camera images (a fixed ``agent'' view and a wrist camera), the 8 joint/gripper readings, and the text instruction ``pick up the book and place it in the back compartment of the caddy''---and outputs a planned sequence of 50 future motor commands, each a 7-dimensional vector (end-effector translation, rotation, gripper). The robot executes only the first command, observes again, and re-plans. So $\pi: o_t \mapsto (a_t, a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+49})$, and the closed loop is $o_{t+1} = \mathrm{sim}(o_t, a_t)$.

Internally the network has two parts. A \emph{vision-language model} encodes images and text into a sequence of vectors. An \emph{action expert}, a smaller transformer, turns those vectors into the 50 commands. The action expert works by \emph{flow matching}: it starts from 50 random Gaussian vectors and refines them in 10 steps into the command sequence, each refinement conditioned on the encoded observation. ``Flow step $t{=}1.0$'' below means the first of those refinement steps.

\subsection{Episodes, success, failure}
An \textbf{episode} starts from one of 50 fixed initial scene configurations (``initial states'', which differ in where the book and the caddy stand), optionally with a \emph{perturbation} applied (the book rotated, the book shifted, or the camera tilted), and runs for up to 520 control steps. The simulator reports \textbf{success} if the book ends up in the caddy's back compartment; otherwise the episode is a \textbf{failure}. On this task, most failures are not dramatic: the policy stalls or mis-grasps and the clock runs out. For each failed episode a fixed rule also assigns a failure \emph{onset} step (when the grasp was missed, the book dropped, or the book left the reachable workspace), used only for the lead-time analysis.

\subsection{Three kinds of information about a running episode}
At each sampled step $t$ (every 5 control steps) we compute a feature vector $x_t$, and a classifier $f_m$ predicts the probability $p_m(t) = f_m(x_t)$ that \emph{this episode will end in failure}. The three models differ only in what goes into $x_t$; the classifier family and its hyper-parameters are identical (gradient-boosted decision trees, fitted once on the Calibration split and frozen).

The nesting is the point. \Mt{} vs \Mo{} asks whether the internals contain failure-relevant information that \emph{no} non-internal source has. \Mt{} vs \Mz{} asks how much the full model gains over an output-only monitor. Note what the nesting does \emph{not} contain: a model with internals but without simulator state. Such a monitor is what one would deploy on a real robot; its accuracy is not measured here.

\subsection{Reading a variable out of activations: the probe}
\label{sec:probe}
Pick one location inside the network---here the 720-dimensional vector entering the fourth layer of the action expert at the first flow step, averaged over the 50 command positions---and call it $h \in \mathbb{R}^{720}$. A \textbf{linear probe} is a ridge regression from $h$ to a target variable. Our variable is the yaw angle $\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}$ between the gripper and the book. Because the book looks the same rotated by $180^\circ$, the target is the doubled angle on the unit circle,
\[
y = \big(\cos 2\theta_{\mathrm{rel}},\; \sin 2\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^2, \qquad
\hat y = W h + b,\quad W \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 720},
\]
fitted by $\min_{W,b} \sum_i \|W h_i + b - y_i\|^2 + \lambda \|W\|_F^2$ on Calibration states and scored by the circular error $|\,\tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(\hat y) - \theta_{\mathrm{rel}}\,|$. A good probe means the network \emph{represents} orientation in a way a linear map can extract. Five candidate locations were preregistered; the rule for choosing one and the achieved error are in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

\begin{claimbox}{Key intuition: decodable $\neq$ used}
A probe finding $\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}$ in $h$ shows only that the information is linearly present. The network might read it through the very direction $W$ spans, through some other direction that is merely correlated with it, or not at all (the information could be a by-product). Telling these apart needs an intervention.
\end{claimbox}

\subsection{Testing whether the variable is used: patching}
The two rows of $W$ span a 2-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{720}$; let $P$ be the orthogonal projector onto it (rank 2). Take a \emph{recipient} state with activation $h_r$ and a \emph{donor} state $h_d$ that is the same in every matched respect (task phase, contact, gripper opening, positions) but whose book is rotated by $30$--$90^\circ$. Replace the recipient's activation by
\[
h_r' \;=\; h_r + \alpha\, P\,(h_d - h_r), \qquad \alpha \in \{0.25,\, 0.5,\, 1.0\},
\]
i.e.\ move only the probe coordinates of $h_r$ a fraction $\alpha$ of the way toward the donor's, leave the other 718 directions untouched, and let the network finish computing its command. If the network reads orientation through $P$, the resulting command should change in the direction appropriate for the \emph{donor's} orientation (the ``target'' component), and not much elsewhere (the ``off-target'' components). Three controls make this a fair test:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Random directions.} Repeat the edit with 1\,000 random rank-2 subspaces $Q$ in place of $P$, scaled to the same shift norm. If $P$ is special, its effect must exceed the random distribution (we use its 95th percentile).
\item \textbf{Matched donor.} Use a donor whose orientation differs by less than $5^\circ$. The edit should then do nothing.
\item \textbf{Staying on the data manifold.} Measure the mean distance from $h_r'$ to its 5 nearest natural Calibration activations; if it exceeds the 95th percentile of that distance among natural states (3.89), the edit has pushed the network into a region it never sees and the result may be meaningless.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How predictions are scored}
\label{sec:metrics}
Each episode $e$ has a label $y_e \in \{0,1\}$ ($1$ = failed) and predictions $p_m(t)$ at the \emph{primary} steps $T_e = \{0, 50, 100, 150, 200\} \cap \{\text{steps before the episode ended}\}$. The episode's log loss under model $m$ is
\[
\ell^m_e = \frac{1}{|T_e|}\sum_{t \in T_e} -\big[\,y_e \log p_m(t) + (1-y_e)\log(1-p_m(t))\,\big],
\]
so every episode carries the same total weight regardless of its length. The \textbf{primary estimand} is the paired mean difference and its relative version,
\[
\Delta = \frac{1}{n}\sum_e \big(\ell^{\Mt}_e - \ell^{\Mo}_e\big), \qquad
\text{lift} = \frac{-\Delta}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_e \ell^{\Mo}_e},
\]
with $n = 158$ valid episodes; the preregistered bar was lift $\geq 3\%$. Two secondary scores: the \textbf{Brier score} $\frac{1}{|T_e|}\sum_t (p_m(t) - y_e)^2$ (lower is better), and the \textbf{AUROC}, the probability that a randomly chosen failed episode receives a higher predicted failure probability than a randomly chosen successful one ($0.5$ = chance, $1$ = perfect ranking).

\textbf{Uncertainty.} Episodes that start from the same initial state are not independent (same scene, different perturbations), so all intervals are \emph{cluster bootstraps}: resample the 20 initial states of the Locked Test with replacement, keep all 8 episodes of each drawn state, recompute the statistic, repeat $10\,000$ times, and report the 5th and 95th percentiles (a 90\% interval). This is the honest sample size: 20 clusters, not 158 episodes and certainly not 9\,988 sampled steps.

\textbf{Alarms and lead time.} A model raises an alarm the first time $p_m(t)$ exceeds a threshold at three consecutive samples. The thresholds were set on Calibration so that at most 10\% of \emph{successful} episodes ever alarm. Lead time is the number of control steps between the alarm and the annotated failure onset; we compare the paired lead of \Mt{} and \Mo{} on the failed episodes.

\section{Why this question}
\label{sec:why}
Interpretability on VLA models has produced decodable representations and steering demonstrations, but rarely a controlled test of whether reading the internals adds \emph{predictive} value over cheaper, non-internal diagnostics. That is the practical bar: a safety monitor built on activations must beat one built on the policy's own outputs and, ideally, one built on privileged information nobody has at deployment.

We designed the test as a nested comparison so that a positive result would be unambiguous and a negative result would still be informative. The ``boring'' alternatives were built in as baselines rather than tested afterwards:
\begin{itemize}
\item If internals only beat outputs (\Mt{} $>$ \Mz{}, \Mt{} $\approx$ \Mo{}), they \emph{substitute} for state you would otherwise need a simulator for---useful, but not new information.
\item If internals beat privileged state (\Mt{} $>$ \Mo{}), they carry information about the policy's own competence that the world state does not.
\item If a probed direction is causally used, moving it should move actions more than random directions of the same size do, and in the predicted direction.
\end{itemize}
Before touching the GPU we wrote down (\texttt{start.md}, \texttt{PREREG.md}) the estimands, the bars, the split logic, and a decision table mapping every outcome to a consequence. Our own toy-model evidence predicted the outcome we then observed: \Mt{} $\approx$ \Mo{} $\gg$ \Mz{} (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}).

\section{Claims}
\label{sec:claims}
All four claims below were preregistered as tests; the tiers are ours.
\begin{claimbox}{Claim 1 (systematic, preregistered primary): internal signals do not improve failure prediction over privileged state.}
Paired log-loss lift \Mt{} vs \Mo{} $= +0.47\%$, 90\% CI $[-1.2\%, +2.1\%]$ ($\Delta$ log loss $-0.0024$, CI $[-0.0111, +0.0063]$), bar $\geq 3\%$. \textbf{Fails.} Evidence: Section~\ref{sec:claim1}.
\end{claimbox}
\begin{claimbox}{Claim 2 (systematic, preregistered secondary): the full model beats the output-only monitor.}
\Mt{} vs \Mz{}: $\Delta$ log loss $-0.120$, CI $[-0.187, -0.052]$; relative lift $18.8\%$. \textbf{Holds}---but since \Mt{} $\supset$ \Mo{}, this measures the value of privileged state plus internals over outputs, not of internals on their own. Section~\ref{sec:claim1}.
\end{claimbox}
\begin{claimbox}{Claim 3 (hedged, preregistered): the probe subspace is not a causal lever at the tested layer and doses.}
Sign-correct $48\%$ [36\%, 62\%]; off-target ratio $4.03$ (bar $\leq 0.25$); 60\% of patched states leave the natural activation manifold. \textbf{Specificity fails at $\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}$.} The stronger multi-layer criterion could not be evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:claim3}).
\end{claimbox}
\begin{claimbox}{Claim 4 (narrow, preregistered): no lead-time gain.}
Median paired lead-time difference \Mt{} $-$ \Mo{} $= 0$ steps, CI $[0,0]$, bar $\geq 5$. \textbf{Fails.} Section~\ref{sec:claim4}.
\end{claimbox}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Policy and task.} \texttt{lerobot/smolvla\_libero} (revision \texttt{31d453f7}), a SmolVLA policy with a SmolVLM2-500M backbone and a flow-matching action expert (10 denoising steps, 50-step action chunks, one executed action per step). Simulator: LIBERO-10 task~5, ``pick up the book and place it in the back compartment of the caddy''; primary object \texttt{black\_book}, planar symmetry order 2. Two 360$\times$360 cameras (agent view, wrist), 8-D proprioceptive state, 7-D relative actions.

\paragraph{Episodes and failure.} Closed loop, replanning every step, horizon 520 control steps after 10 settle steps. Success is LIBERO's own \texttt{check\_success}; failure is no success by step 520. A deterministic, frozen annotator assigns each failed episode a failure \emph{onset} step (missed grasp, dropped object, irrecoverable workspace exit, or terminal horizon), used only for lead time.

\paragraph{Perturbation conditions.} Eight cells of 20 episodes each, one per initial state: unperturbed (iid); the book rotated by $-37.5^\circ, -22.5^\circ, +22.5^\circ, +37.5^\circ$; the book displaced 4.5\,cm diagonally; the agent camera yawed $\pm 5^\circ$ with a 2\,cm lateral shift. The Calibration split used different rotation angles ($\pm 15/30/45^\circ$) and a 3\,cm axis displacement, so Locked Test conditions are held-out perturbations, not just held-out seeds.

\paragraph{Splits.} Fifty fixed initial states: 0--9 Discovery (stack reproduction, reality gate), 10--29 Calibration (all fitting and selection), 30--49 Locked Test. Reset seeds are derived from split, init, and cell; nothing crosses splits. Uncertainty is always a cluster bootstrap over the 20 init-state clusters (10\,000 replicates, 90\% percentile intervals, seed 260803), never over frames.

\paragraph{The three predictors.} All three are histogram-gradient-boosting classifiers (learning rate 0.03, 200 trees, 7 leaves, minimum 20 samples per leaf). The family and hyper-parameters were chosen on Calibration by grouped 5-fold out-of-fold \Mo{} log loss over a small preregistered grid (logistic regression with $C \in \{0.01,\dots,100\}$ or boosting), then frozen; a Platt calibrator was fitted on the out-of-fold predictions. What each model sees is defined in Section~\ref{sec:plain}: \Mz{} 13 output-only features, \Mo{} those plus simulator state and coverage, \Mt{} those plus 8 internal features. No \Mt{} feature uses simulator ground truth.

\paragraph{The probe.} As defined in Section~\ref{sec:probe}. Five preregistered candidate locations (VLM residual at layer 12; action-expert residual after layers 3 and 11, at flow steps $t{=}1.0$ and $0.5$). Selection rule: episode-weighted ridge regression, grouped 5-fold by init, circular MAE, lowest mean then preregistered preference within one SE. Selected: \texttt{early\_expert\_t1\_0} (width 720, mean over the 50 action tokens), MAE $0.253$\,rad vs. $0.332$ for a constant predictor and $0.315$ for proprioception alone. The probe is real but modest: it tracks about 15\% of a $15^\circ$ object rotation (median error $12.8^\circ$) on Calibration.

\paragraph{Causal test.} The patch and its three controls are defined in Section~\ref{sec:plain}. Donors are matched on phase and contact, gripper opening within 1\,cm, gripper and book positions within 2\,cm, normalised time within 0.10, and book orientation $30$--$90^\circ$ apart; the patch is applied to all 50 command positions at the selected layer and flow step, and the policy's first command is read. Sixty pairs (3 outcome-blind seeds $\times$ 20), $\alpha$ frozen at $0.25$ on Calibration (smallest of $\{0.25,0.5,1\}$ meeting the off-manifold cap), $\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0\}$ as sensitivity.

\paragraph{Blinding and single evaluation.} Locked Test labels were never read by a human or by any fitting code before the evaluator ran; the run was monitored by counts and hashes only. Two loader defects (a trailing newline in a frozen lock file; a read-only mapping type) aborted the evaluator at input validation before any metric existed; each was fixed, tested, and recorded as an amendment before the rerun (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\section{Claims 1 and 2: internals add nothing to privileged state}
\label{sec:claim1}
\paragraph{Why this experiment.} The primary comparison isolates the incremental value of internals over \emph{everything} non-internal; the secondary isolates their value over what a deployed monitor could actually see. The four possible outcomes were mapped to consequences in advance (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}).

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:models} and Figure~\ref{fig:headline}. \Mo{} and \Mt{} are equivalent on every metric; both dominate \Mz{}. The primary interval on the relative lift, $[-1.2\%, +2.1\%]$, excludes the 3\% bar; its favourable end is 0.9 percentage points short of it. The \Mt{} vs \Mz{} interval excludes zero on the favourable side.

\paragraph{Boring explanations we checked.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Overfitting on Calibration would hide a real gap.} It does not: the Calibration $\to$ Locked Test drop is similar for \Mo{} and \Mt{} (AUROC $0.937 \to 0.831$ and $0.938 \to 0.835$; Figure~\ref{fig:gap}), and their Calibration out-of-fold lift was already only $0.40\%$.
\item \emph{Underpowered.} The bar was set at a 3\% relative lift; the observed 90\% interval has half-width $1.7\%$ and its upper end is $2.1\%$. A true 3\% effect would most likely have shown, but a 1--2\% effect is compatible with the data; the preregistered bar, not the point estimate, is what fails.
\item \emph{The internal features are broken.} The probe they are built on beats every control on Calibration (circular MAE 0.253\,rad vs.\ 0.332 constant, 0.315 proprioception-only, 0.406 shuffled labels), and removing the \Mo{} coverage features raises the Calibration lift of \Mt{} over \Mo{} from 0.40\% to 1.20\%---still below the bar (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}). The features carry orientation information; it is simply redundant with the state block.
\item \emph{Kill switch.} A preregistered kill switch (any non-internal model with AUROC $\geq 0.95$ on Calibration would make the task too easy) did not trigger.
\end{itemize}

\section{Claim 3: the decodable direction is a readout, not a lever}
\label{sec:claim3}
\paragraph{Why this experiment.} A linear probe finding a variable proves decodability, not use. The patching test asks whether \emph{this} 2-D direction is one the policy's action expert reads from. Possible outcomes: specific and sign-correct (mechanism), sign-correct but unspecific (the direction overlaps something used but is not the used coordinate), or neither (readout only).

\paragraph{Result.} Figure~\ref{fig:causal}, Table~\ref{tab:causal}. Of 60 attempted pairs, 52 were valid (eight lacked an eligible donor or matched control; $\geq 30$ needed). The patch is small in absolute terms (median donor-aligned effect $-1.5\times 10^{-4}$ in standardised action units) and, crucially, \emph{smaller than random}: the 95th percentile of the 1\,000 norm-matched random subspaces is $1.0\times 10^{-4}$, giving a median off-target ratio of $4.03$ against a bar of $0.25$. The sign of the action change matched the donor's orientation in $25/52$ pairs ($48\%$, CI $[36\%, 62\%]$), the matched-donor control---which should be inert---moved the action in the ``predicted'' direction $54\%$ of the time, and only one of three seeds was positive. Sixty percent of patched states fell outside the natural activation manifold ($5$-NN distance above the Calibration 95th percentile), so the small effects are not explained by an overly cautious dose.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The probe direction is decodable because orientation information is present, but the action expert does not read it through this coordinate at this layer---moving it is indistinguishable from noise. Two readings remain open: the used coordinate is elsewhere (different layer, token, or a non-linear function of several directions), or orientation enters the action through the visual pathway in a way that a residual-stream patch at one layer cannot reproduce. Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives our odds.

\section{Claim 4: no earlier alarm}
\label{sec:claim4}
Thresholds were fixed on Calibration at a 10\% episode-level false-positive rate (\Mz{} 0.541, \Mo{} 0.493, \Mt{} 0.504; alarm = 3 consecutive exceedances at 5-step cadence). On the 60 failed Locked Test episodes, \Mo{} and \Mt{} each detect 59; median lead before the annotated onset is 435 and 422.5 steps respectively, and the \emph{paired} median difference is $0$ with a degenerate interval $[0,0]$: in most failed episodes both alarms fire at the same early step. The large absolute leads reflect that most failures on this task are ``terminal horizon'' failures (on Calibration, 48 of 53) that the state-based models flag almost from the start, not a late, sudden break. \paragraph{Result.} A logistic regression on the raw 720-dimensional activation vector (regularisation chosen by inner grouped cross-validation) ranks failures about as well as \Mo{} (AUROC 0.940 vs 0.949); combined with the output features it matches \Mo{} on AUROC (0.951) and comes within 8\% in log loss (paired lift $-7.9\%$, CI $[-15.7\%, +0.2\%]$). Against the output-only monitor the gain is large and clear (log-loss lift $+28\%$, CI $[+20\%, +36\%]$). The eight interpretability-motivated features of \Mt{}---which describe how the orientation probe \emph{behaves}---are much weaker on their own (AUROC 0.874--0.894): the failure information is in the activation, not in the probe's consistency. Re-fitting \Mt{} vs \Mo{} here reproduces the preregistered null ($-0.0\%$, CI $[-1.7\%, +1.6\%]$).

\paragraph{What this changes and what it does not.} It supports the reading ``internals can largely substitute for privileged state'' that the nested design could not test, and it says the substitution works through the raw representation rather than through the geometric features we designed. It does not change the preregistered conclusions: internals still add nothing on top of state, and the probe direction is still not causal. Caveats: post-hoc; evaluated on the split where the probe location and hyper-parameters were chosen (optimistic); 20 clusters; a 720-dimensional linear model on 160 episodes can overfit despite nested regularisation; the direct probe is a correlate, not a mechanism; performance on the Locked Test and under real-robot distribution shift is unknown.

\section{Limitations and what would change our mind}
\label{sec:limits}
We separate three readings of the negative results and give our rough odds; these are judgments, not measurements.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hypothesis false for this policy/task (\Mt{} adds nothing over \Mo{}): $\sim 75\%$.} The interval is tight, the Calibration out-of-fold lift was equally small, and the per-cell pattern is uniform. We would update if a different internal feature set (e.g.\ a probe for gripper--object contact or for the goal compartment) produced a lift $\geq 3\%$ under the same protocol.
\item \textbf{Method insensitive for the causal claim (wrong layer/coordinate, not absence of mechanism): $\sim 40\%$.} The failure is specific: we tested one linear 2-D subspace at one residual location and flow step, with a patch that is smaller than random noise. We would update toward ``mechanism exists'' if patching the VLM-context token or the late expert location, or a non-linear (e.g.\ token-wise) version of the direction, produced a specific, sign-correct effect with the same controls. We would update toward ``no mechanism'' if a probe with much lower MAE at any location still failed specificity.
\item \textbf{Underpowered: $\sim 15\%$ for the prediction claims; $\sim 25\%$ for the causal sign test.} The primary interval excludes the 3\% bar, but a 1--2\% lift would need roughly four times the episodes to resolve. The causal sign interval ($\pm 13$\,pp) could hide a weak true effect, but the specificity failure does not depend on power.
\end{itemize}
Further limits: the nested design leaves the most deployment-relevant question open---no model with internals but \emph{without} simulator state was fitted, so ``internals can replace privileged state'' is a hypothesis this study did not test (fitting \Mz{}\,+\,internals on Calibration out-of-fold would take minutes and no GPU; evaluating it on the Locked Test would be a labelled post-hoc analysis). One policy, one task, 158 episodes; failure labels come from the simulator, not human judgment; the annotated failure \emph{onset} is a deterministic heuristic; the probe is modest (tracks $\sim 15\%$ of a rotation); and the study cannot say anything about SAE-style features or non-linear readouts, which were out of scope by design.

\paragraph{What we would do next, if anything.} Not another run of this design. The informative follow-ups are (i) the missing model, outputs\,+\,internals without simulator state---done on Calibration in Section~\ref{sec:explore}, promising, and still to be tried as an explicitly post-hoc Locked Test analysis; (ii) a cross-layer causal sweep with the same controls, cheap now that the pipeline exists ($\approx$\,3 GPU-hours per layer); and (iii) replacing the orientation probe with a probe for the variable the policy actually fails on (contact/grasp), which the per-cell results suggest.

\section{Deviations and amendments during the Locked Test}
\label{app:deviations}
All recorded prospectively in \texttt{AMENDMENTS.md} before the affected step ran; none touched a scientific quantity.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Replacement instance.} The original GPU instance had been destroyed by the provider (negative balance) before the Locked Test; the frozen runtime was rebuilt from pinned wheels, source hashes and model snapshots on a new RTX~5090 (\texttt{ops/locked\_test\_bootstrap.sh}); the machine-readable preflight matched the freeze on every field.

\end{itemize}

\section{Glossary}
\label{app:gloss}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[Policy] The neural network that maps an observation (images, joint readings, instruction) to motor commands. Here: SmolVLA, a vision-language model plus a flow-matching action expert.
\item[Episode] One attempt at the task from a fixed initial state, up to 520 control steps; ends in success or failure.
\item[Activation] Any intermediate vector the network computes; we use one 720-dimensional vector at a fixed location.
\item[Probe] A linear regression from an activation to a variable of interest (here the gripper--book yaw), used to test whether the variable is linearly represented.
\item[Patching] Editing an activation during the forward pass and observing the change in output; used to test whether a represented variable is actually used.
\item[Privileged state] The simulator's exact knowledge of all poses and contacts, unavailable to a real robot.
\item[Counterfactual transformation] Re-rendering the same scene with a small change (brightness, camera yaw, book rotation or shift) and re-running the policy, to measure how sensitive its output is.
\item[Flow matching / flow step] The action expert refines random noise into commands in 10 steps; flow step $t{=}1.0$ is the first.
\item[Calibration / Locked Test] The split on which everything is fitted and frozen, and the split that is collected only afterwards and evaluated exactly once.
\item[Cluster bootstrap] Resampling whole initial states (with all their episodes) to obtain confidence intervals that respect the dependence between episodes.
\item[Off-target ratio] Size of the output change outside the predicted direction divided by the size along it; a specific intervention has a small ratio.
\item[Off-manifold] An edited activation that is farther from all natural activations than 95\% of natural activations are from each other.
\end{description}
